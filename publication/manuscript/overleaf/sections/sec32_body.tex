The three one-dimensional families -- the data-driven MLP, the strong-form PINN and the weak-form VPINN -- share the network, the optimiser, the epoch limit, the early-stopping rule, the mini-batch size and the seeds, and differ only in the composition of the loss (Section~\ref{sec:models}). Accuracy is reported as macro-RMSE, the mean of the four component RMSEs in MPa, as mean~$\pm$~standard deviation over five seeds. Differences between families are tested with a paired $t$-test over seeds \citep{demsar2006} and called established at $p<0.05$. The difference itself, the macro-RMSE of a physics-informed family minus that of the MLP averaged over the seeds, is denoted $\Delta$ in this section. It is a difference of errors in MPa, negative where the physics-informed family is more accurate, and is unrelated to the inhomogeneity parameter $\Delta$ of Section~\ref{sec:res_fem_verification}. Because $\tau_{rz}$ is two orders of magnitude smaller than the normal components, a coefficient of determination over the normal components only, $R^2_{\mathrm{n}}$, is given alongside. It is taken with a single global denominator, the variance of each component over the whole dataset, and where a per-component $R^2$ or NRMSE below uses the held-out slice as denominator, this is stated at the number. % EDIT: denominator named
Throughout this section the finite-element solution serves as the reference field, and this sets the domain of applicability of the results reported below. They characterise the surrogate models with respect to that reference, namely their accuracy on it, their transferability beyond the training range and their robustness to defects of the source. Table~\ref{tab:curves} shows how the accuracy changes with the size of the training set. It is often expected that physical constraints in the loss are most useful when the data are scarce, and the present results do not confirm this. On the random split the gap between the PINN and the MLP narrows as the training set grows, from $-0.61$ to $-0.10$~MPa, in line with that expectation, but on the semi-angle extrapolation it changes in the opposite direction, from $+0.24$ to $-0.54$~MPa. Of the eight individual comparisons only two are significant, at 50\,\% of the data with $p=0.048$ and at 25\,\% with $p=0.033$, and they have opposite signs, as may happen by chance. At the smallest fraction the gap is not significant on either split. The claim that the physics term is most useful when the data are scarce is therefore not made.

The strong-form PINN was trained in two independent implementations of the same protocol, in PyTorch and in JAX, on the random split and the semi-angle extrapolation, five seeds each. The engines were checked first: on bit-identical weights and inputs the derivative $\partial\sigma_{rr}/\partial r$ from \texttt{torch.autograd} and from \texttt{jax.grad} agrees to $8.7\times10^{-15}$ absolute ($9.8\times10^{-16}$ relative), i.e.\ to machine precision. A DeepXDE implementation \citep{lu2021} of the same network runs on the PyTorch backend and differentiates with the same \texttt{torch.autograd.grad}, so the comparison involves two automatic-differentiation engines, not three.

The comparison with the source itself rests on the single-seed training, in which the source was evaluated on the same 261 held-out cases as the three families. Figures~\ref{fig:compare-fields} and \ref{fig:residual-maps} show the fields of that training for the case with the median error of the full PINN rather than the best. The predicted stress fields of the three families are visually alike, whereas their residual fields are not. Within each component the colour scale is shared by the FEM reference and all families, so that the panels of one column are directly comparable, and the radial axis is stretched, the window being about 3.5 radii long.

In this comparison the residuals are made dimensionless. The equilibrium residuals $R_r$, $R_z$ of Section~\ref{sec:models} are divided by the scales of the loss term \eqref{eq:lphys}, $s_{R_1}=(s_{rr}+s_{\theta\theta})/R_{\mathrm{char}}$ and $s_{R_z}=s_{zz}/L_{\mathrm{char}}+s_{rz}/R_{\mathrm{char}}$, computed once from the FEM data and applied identically to the source and all families, since otherwise each family would be normalised by itself and the values would not be comparable. A value of 0.1 means that the equation fails to close by 10\,\% of the characteristic magnitude of its own terms. Medians are taken over the interior grid nodes of all held-out cases together, where the central difference is second-order accurate. At the edges of the grid it degenerates to a one-sided difference.

In that training the macro-RMSE, the mean of the four component RMSEs, was 7.52~MPa for the MLP against 7.78 and 7.73~MPa for the reduced and full forms of the PINN, a spread of 0.26~MPa in favour of the MLP. The median radial residual was 0.041 for the source, 0.115 for the MLP and 0.064 and 0.062 for the reduced and full forms. The MLP thus violated radial equilibrium 2.8~times more strongly than the finite-element source it was trained on, and both PINNs about 1.5~times. A residual of 0.115 means that the radial equation fails to close by 11.5\,\% of the characteristic magnitude of its own terms. The axial residual was 0.091 for the source and 0.074 for the MLP, and it fell markedly only with the full form, to 0.047, against 0.064 for the reduced one. The traction-free violation $|\sigma_{rr}(r{=}1)|$ was 3.54~MPa for the source and 2.90~MPa for the MLP, which thus satisfied the condition at the level of the source, whereas the reduced and full PINNs, at 0.78 and 0.56~MPa, satisfied it 4.5 and 6.3~times more strictly than the data they were trained on. Since the finite-element field is affected by nodal extrapolation and averaging, a surrogate that violates the boundary condition by less than its source is, in this specific sense, more physically admissible than the data.

The corruption experiment of Table~\ref{tab:corrupt} yields the strongest and most interpretable effect of the physics term. On clean labels the PINN and the MLP are indistinguishable ($\Delta=-0.10$~MPa, $p=0.72$): the term adds nothing where data and equations already agree. When 1, 5 and 10\,\% of the training cases carry corrupted labels, the gap $\Delta$ widens monotonically to $-0.48$, $-1.11$ and $-1.59$~MPa. Both errors are measured on the clean hold-out and both grow with the corrupted fraction, that of the MLP faster, so that the increasingly negative gap is a growing advantage of the PINN. This advantage is established at 5 and 10\,\% ($p=0.010$ and $0.037$) but not at 1\,\% ($p=0.10$). A regression of the gap on the corrupted fraction over the twelve seed-level points (four fractions, three seeds each) gives $-14.2$~MPa per unit fraction ($p=0.00036$, $R^2=0.74$): the advantage grows by about 1.4~MPa for every 10\,\% of corrupted labels. At 10\,\% the MLP has degraded by a factor of 1.81 and the PINN by 1.64. The weak form (1.79) is not protected in the same way. The mechanism is the one seen in Table~\ref{tab:admissibility}: the strong-form residual and the traction-free term pull the solution towards an admissible field, and the pull matters exactly when the labels contradict it. Taken together, Tables~\ref{tab:admissibility} and \ref{tab:corrupt} locate the benefit of the physics term not in accuracy against a clean finite-element field, where it is marginal, but in the admissibility of the solution and its resistance to defects of the data source.

\paragraph{Limitations}
The focus of this work is on surrogate models built by machine learning and on the finite-element simulation that supplies their training data. The adequacy of the finite-element model was assessed by comparison with analytical solutions for the drawing stress and by internal consistency tests, as described in Section~\ref{sec:res_fem_verification}, and the surrogates are evaluated with respect to that model: their accuracy on it, their transferability beyond the training range and their robustness to defects of the data source. A broader analysis tied directly to industrial drawing equipment requires additional verification by full-scale experiments, which lie outside the scope of this study. % src: MANUSCRIPT.md §3
The factorial grid is incomplete and unbalanced: the level $v = 5$~m/min is represented by 122 cases against 554--607 for the other levels, each batch of simulations holds a single level of speed (Section~\ref{sec:prep-data}), and 13.2\,\% of the raw cases are bit-identical duplicates of another case differing only in the friction label, so friction cannot be used as an extrapolation axis. % src: MANUSCRIPT.md §3; ERRATA.md E-12 (122 vs 554–607), E-06 (13.2 %); VALIDATION.md §6 (speed = batch)
