Verification of the simulation results was carried out by comparison with the established
analytical solutions for the drawing stress, those of Siebel
\citep{siebel1943erwaermung,siebel1947drahtziehen} and of Avitzur
\citep{avitzur1963small,avitzur1964large}, the latter both in its friction-factor form and in the
Coulomb form on which Vega et~al.\ \citep{vega2009temperature} base their modification, and by
internal consistency tests of the exported fields.

% with the analytical comparison the review asks for.

The drawing stress cannot be read from the cases used for the surrogates, whose exported frame is
taken after the wire has left the die and the section is unloaded (median
$|\int\sigma_{zz}\,\mathrm{d}S|/S$ of 5.3~MPa against an rms $\sigma_{zz}$ of 167~MPa).
% src: VALIDATION.md §7 (5.3 / 167 MPa on the 1756 retained cases)
It is obtained instead from a dedicated series of runs of the same model, in which the reaction at
the die reference point and the coordinates, equivalent plastic strain and temperature of a
radial row of material points are written as histories in time over the whole pass. The series
covers a reduction of 10\,\% in diameter at die semi-angles of $8^\circ$, $12^\circ$ and
$16^\circ$, calibration ratios from 0 to 1, drawing speeds of 10 and 20~m/min and all three friction
coefficients, together with a reduction of 15\,\% at $8^\circ$ without a bearing. In total, 112 runs are
used, 106 of the 108 combinations at 10\,\% and the six at 15\,\%.
% src: publication/reexport/verification_recalc.py on the export of 28.09.2026 (res_recalc, 112
% files; 106 at Q = 10 %, 6 at Q = 15 %), with 1020_2a_16_rd_1000_cal_0_v_10_f_0100 re-exported on
% 02.10.2026 (its first version carried the wire histories of ..._f_050); per-run results in
% publication/reexport/results/recalc_2026-09-28.json, every number of this section printed by
% publication/reexport/sec31_numbers.py (results/sec31_numbers_2026-10-02.txt). Not in the export:

The axial reaction is averaged over the steady phase of the pass, in which the wire and the die
are in contact and both the deformation process and the deformation zone are stable. The phase
is found from the signal itself, and within it the reaction varies by 1.9--4.4\,\% about its
mean. The drawing stress is then
% src: drawing_force.steady_window (plateau from the middle of the pass, +-5 % of the level,
% 5 mm margins; e.g. 34-137 mm of die travel at alpha = 8, 38-141 mm at 16 deg); margins of
% 0-10 mm, smoothing over 5-20 mm and a tolerance of 3-7 % move sigma_d by at most 0.2 % wherever
% the plateau is found (at 3 % it is not found in 9 runs).
\begin{equation}
\sigma_d = \frac{F}{S}, \qquad S = \pi R_f^2,
\label{eq:sigma-d}
\end{equation}
where $F$ is the time-averaged axial reaction and $S$ the cross-section of the drawn wire, with
the outlet radius $R_f$ taken as reached in the model rather than from the nominal reduction.
For a typical run, at a reduction of 10\,\% in
diameter, a die semi-angle of $12^\circ$, a calibration ratio of 0.5, a drawing speed of 10~m/min and
$\mu = 0.05$, the drawing stress is 121.7~MPa. The values for all runs are summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:drawstress}.
% src: verification_recalc.py (typical run 1020_2a_24_rd_1000_cal_50_v_10_f_050: 121.7 MPa at
% Rf = 16.07 mm); surface PEEQ keeps rising for 10-20 mm past the cone exit.

The first two relations compared below are the classical ones. In the slab-method form of Siebel,
\begin{equation}
\sigma_d = \sigma_f\left[\ln\frac{1}{1-r}\,(1+\mu\cot\alpha) + \frac{2}{3}\,\alpha\right],
\label{eq:siebel}
\end{equation}
with $r = 1-(R_f/R_0)^2$ the reduction in area, $\alpha$ the die semi-angle and $\sigma_f$ the flow
stress, and, in the upper-bound form of Avitzur,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\sigma_d = \sigma_f\biggl[\,&2f(\alpha)\ln\frac{R_0}{R_f}
  + \frac{2}{\sqrt3}\,\frac{\alpha-\sin\alpha\cos\alpha}{\sin^2\alpha}\\
  &+ \frac{2}{\sqrt3}\,m\cot\alpha\ln\frac{R_0}{R_f}
  + \frac{2}{\sqrt3}\,m\,\frac{L_b}{R_f}\biggr],
\end{split}
\label{eq:avitzur}
\end{equation}
whose four terms are the ideal deformation work, the redundant shear, the friction on the cone and
the friction on the bearing. Here $f(\alpha)$ is the closed form of the deformation integral,
% src: code/analytical.py f_avitzur against f_avitzur_numeric (agreement to eight digits)
$f\to1$ as $\alpha\to0$, so that the first
term reduces to $\ln[1/(1-r)]$, $m$ is the friction factor and $L_b$ the bearing length
\citep{siebel1947drahtziehen,avitzur1968metal,avitzur1971study}. In the model
$L_b = kD_0 = 36k$~mm (Section~\ref{sec:materials}), so that $L_b/R_f$ is 2.2--2.3 times the
calibration ratio.
% src: bearing length measured in Abaqus CAE on 1020_2a_16_rd_1000_cal_100_v_10_f_050: 0.036 m
% (30.09.2026). The exports agree: die RP initial z = c - 36.000 k mm at every alpha; bearing-only
% tail phase 36.0 k mm (88 runs); 36 mm at k = 1 in the earlier export at Q = 1.5 %. The generator
% line cp_len = 2 r_1 cp (32.4 mm at k = 1, Q = 10 %) is not what the die profile uses.

Equation~\eqref{eq:avitzur} measures the friction through the factor $m$, the ratio of the
interface shear stress to the shear yield stress, whereas the simulations are driven by a Coulomb
coefficient $\mu$. The Siebel relation above already takes $\mu$ directly. The conversion
$m = \sqrt3\,\mu$ holds only where the normal pressure on the contact equals the flow stress, so it
is an assumption about the contact rather than a change of units. Avitzur gives the bound in a
second form in which $\mu$ enters directly and the bearing is treated as a zone along which the
axial stress grows under Coulomb friction \citep{avitzur1968metal,avitzur1971study}. In the writing
of Vega et~al.\ \citep{vega2009temperature,vega2009investigation,haddi2011analysis}, who
build their modification on it, it reads
\begin{equation}
\frac{\sigma_d}{\sigma_f} = \frac{\begin{aligned}
  &2f(\alpha)\ln\frac{R_0}{R_f}
  + \frac{2}{\sqrt3}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sin^2\alpha}-\cot\alpha\right)\\
  &+ 2\mu\left[\cot\alpha\left(1-\ln\frac{R_0}{R_f}\right)\ln\frac{R_0}{R_f}
  + \frac{L_b}{R_f}\right]\end{aligned}}
  {\displaystyle 1+2\mu\,\frac{L_b}{R_f}},
\label{eq:avitzur-vega}
\end{equation}
written here for zero back tension and with the flow stress in place of the yield stress of the
original. The denominator is Avitzur's treatment of the bearing
\citep{avitzur1964large,avitzur1968metal}. It is what the axial force balance on a cylindrical
bearing under Coulomb friction gives when the normal pressure follows from the yield condition,
$p = \sigma_f-\sigma_z$, and is evaluated at the exit stress:
$\sigma_z(L_b) = \sigma_z(0) + 2\mu\,(L_b/R_f)\,[\sigma_f-\sigma_z(L_b)]$, which rearranges to the form
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:avitzur-vega}. Integrating the same balance along the bearing instead gives
$\sigma_z(L_b) = \sigma_f-[\sigma_f-\sigma_z(0)]\exp(-2\mu L_b/R_f)$. The two agree to first order in
$2\mu L_b/R_f$, and at the present parameters the integrated form lies above
Eq.~\eqref{eq:avitzur-vega} by at most 4.4\,\%. Both forms thus carry an explicit assumption about
the pressure on the bearing.

The modification those authors propose is to drop the assumption that $\mu$ is a constant of the
lubricant and make it a function of the temperature measured by a thermocouple set in the die above
the bearing, which they take to be proportional to the temperature of the wire--die interface,
\begin{equation}
\mu = \mu_0\left(\frac{T}{T_0}\right)^{m_\mu},
\label{eq:mu-temperature}
\end{equation}
with $\mu_0$ the coefficient at a reference temperature $T_0$.

Inverting Eq.~\eqref{eq:avitzur-vega} against their own force measurements, they obtain
$\mu_0 = 0.0125$,
$m_\mu = 2.68$ and $T_0 = 15.6\,^\circ$C for copper drawn in oil at 1--7~m/s, the temperatures
entering the ratio in degrees Celsius. In the present simulations $\mu$ is prescribed and constant,
so Eq.~\eqref{eq:mu-temperature} has no counterpart in the model. What it contributes to the
comparison is stated below.

For each run the three relations are evaluated with one and the same flow stress, obtained from
the constitutive equation~\eqref{eq:johnson-cook} by averaging it along the strain, strain-rate
and temperature histories exported for that run (Section~\ref{sec:materials}). It lies between
314 and 331~MPa depending on the run, and no parameter is fitted. Along these histories the equivalent plastic
strain reaches 0.22--0.34 on the axis, 0.27--0.40 at the surface and 0.30--0.46 at its maximum,
1--2~mm below the surface of the billet. The strain rate, taken as the 95th percentile of each
node's history, reaches 4.5--16~s$^{-1}$ and the surface temperature 47--98~$^\circ$C. The flow
stress averaged at the individual nodes lies between 306 and 338~MPa over all runs and varies
across the section by at most 15~MPa within a run. Within a run $\sigma_f$
multiplies each relation as a whole, so the columns of Table~\ref{tab:drawstress} differ only in
the bracketed work terms, and an error in $\sigma_f$ would shift all three predictions of that
run by the same factor.
Each row of Table~\ref{tab:drawstress} is the mean over the calibration ratios at 10~m/min, of six
runs at the reduction of 10\,\% (five in the rows $12^\circ$, $\mu = 0.050$ and $16^\circ$,
$\mu = 0.025$) and of one at 15\,\%, and the finite-element values averaged in a row differ by at
most 0.4~MPa. Below the rows, Table~\ref{tab:drawstress} gives for each relation the mean absolute

The flow stress and the temperature rise can also be estimated from the geometry of the pass,
with the exit radius $R_f$ reached in the model as the only quantity taken from the finite-element
solution:
\begin{align}
\Delta &= \frac{R_0+R_f}{R_0-R_f}\,\sin\alpha, \label{eq:delta}\\
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{hom}} &= 2\ln\frac{R_0}{R_f}, \qquad
\Phi = 0.8 + \frac{\Delta}{4.4}, \qquad
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}} = \Phi\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{hom}}, \label{eq:closed-strain}\\
\edot &= \frac{6\,v\,R_f^2\tan\alpha\,\ln(R_0/R_f)}{R_0^3-R_f^3}, \qquad
\langle\sigma_f\rangle = A + \frac{B\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\,n}}{n+1}, \label{eq:closed-rate}\\
\Delta T &= \frac{\beta\,\sigma_d}{\rho c}, \label{eq:closed-rest}
\end{align}
where $\Delta$ is the inhomogeneity parameter of the deformation zone, the ratio of its mean
diameter to the length of contact along the die cone, $L$ in Fig.~\ref{fig:scheme}, set by the die semi-angle and the entry and
exit radii, $\Phi$ the excess-work factor, $\edot$ the mean strain rate over the time taken to
traverse the cone at constant volume flow, and $\langle\sigma_f\rangle$ the mean of the strain
term of the constitutive equation over the strain from zero to $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}$
\citep{backofen1972deformation}. The
flow stress of the estimate is this mean multiplied by the rate and temperature factors of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:johnson-cook}, evaluated at $\edot$ and at the initial temperature plus half the rise
$\Delta T$, and its drawing stress is Eq.~\eqref{eq:avitzur} with $m = \sqrt3\,\mu$ and without the
bearing term. The
excess-work correlation is quoted in the literature in more than one form. Replacing
$\Phi = 0.8+\Delta/4.4$ by the alternative $\Phi = 0.88+0.12\,\Delta$ changes $\Phi$ by a factor of
1.1 to 1.3 but the flow stress, and the estimated temperature rise, by only 0.3 to 1.0\,\%,
because the hardening term of the constitutive equation is small against $A$. The
choice therefore does not carry the comparison. The temperature
does not need a separate source, because it closes on the deformation work itself: that work per
unit volume is the part of $\sigma_d$ raised in the cone, a fraction $\beta$ of which becomes
heat, so the mean rise is $\Delta T = \beta\,\sigma_d/(\rho c)$. Since $\sigma_d$ depends on the
flow stress and the flow stress on the temperature, this is a fixed point, reached in at most
seven iterations. The friction on the bearing is left out of this balance, since the drawing stress
of these runs does not depend on the bearing, as shown below. For the
112 runs the fixed point returns a flow stress of 313--336~MPa, in agreement with the
314--331~MPa used in Table~\ref{tab:drawstress}, at $\Delta = 1.7$--4.7,
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0.30$--0.45 and $\edot = 2.4$--10.0~s$^{-1}$. The closed
estimate is quoted with its own value throughout, and no column of Table~\ref{tab:drawstress}
uses it.
