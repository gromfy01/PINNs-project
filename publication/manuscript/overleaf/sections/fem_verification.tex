\label{sec:res_fem_verification}
Verification of the simulation results was carried out by comparison with the established
analytical solutions for the drawing stress, those of Siebel
\citep{siebel1943erwaermung,siebel1947drahtziehen} and of Avitzur
\citep{avitzur1963small,avitzur1964large} and the modification of the latter due to Vega et~al.\ \citep{vega2009temperature}, and by internal consistency
tests of the exported fields.
% NOTE:, K18: the section no longer opens with "in place of experimental validation"; it opens
% with the analytical comparison the review asks for.

The drawing stress cannot be read from the cases used for the surrogates, whose exported frame is
taken after the wire has left the die and the section is unloaded (median
$|\int\sigma_{zz}\,\mathrm{d}A|/A$ of 5.3~MPa against an rms $\sigma_{zz}$ of 167~MPa).
% src: VALIDATION.md §7 (5.3 / 167 MPa on the 1756 retained cases)
It is obtained instead from a dedicated series of runs of the same model, in which the reaction at
the die reference point and the coordinates, equivalent plastic strain and temperature of a
radial row of material points are written as histories in time over the whole pass. The series
covers a reduction of 10\,\% in diameter at die semi-angles of $8^\circ$, $12^\circ$ and
$16^\circ$, land ratios from 0 to 1, drawing speeds of 10 and 20~m/min and all three friction
coefficients, together with a reduction of 15\,\% at $8^\circ$ without a land. In total, 111 runs are
used. \pending{One run, $\alpha=8^\circ$, $k=0$, $v=10$~m/min, $\mu=0.1$, is excluded until it is
exported again: its reaction belongs to $\mu=0.1$ but its material-point histories are a copy of
the $\mu=0.05$ run. Two runs of the grid are missing from the export.}
% src: publication/reexport/verification_recalc.py on the export of 28.09.2026 (res_recalc, 112
% files; 106 at Q = 10 %, 6 at Q = 15 %); EXCLUDE = 1020_2a_16_rd_1000_cal_0_v_10_f_0100 (wire
% columns identical to ..._f_050); missing: 1020_2a_24_rd_1000_cal_10_v_10_f_050 and
% 1020_2a_32_rd_1000_cal_30_v_10_f_025.
The axial reaction is averaged over the steady phase of the pass, in which the wire and the die
are in contact and both the deformation process and the deformation zone are stable. The phase
is found from the signal itself, and within it the reaction varies by 1.9--4.4\,\% about its
mean. The drawing stress is then
% src: drawing_force.steady_window (plateau from the middle of the pass, +-5 % of the level,
% 5 mm margins; e.g. 34-137 mm of die travel at alpha = 8, 38-141 mm at 16 deg); changing the
% window moves sigma_d by at most 0.4 %.
\begin{equation}
\sigma_d = \frac{F}{S}, \qquad S = \pi R_f^2,
\label{eq:sigma-d}
\end{equation}
where $F$ is the time-averaged axial reaction and $S$ the cross-section of the drawn wire, with
the outlet radius $R_f$ taken as reached in the model rather than from the nominal reduction:
the wire keeps thinning for a short distance after it leaves the die cone, and the reduction
reached is 10.2--11.5\,\% instead of the nominal 10\,\%, and 15.1--15.5\,\% instead of
15\,\%. \pending{Whether this continued thinning is physical or a feature of the model set-up
is to be confirmed by the model author.} For a typical run, at a reduction of 10\,\% in
diameter, a die semi-angle of $12^\circ$, a land ratio of 0.5, a drawing speed of 10~m/min and
$\mu = 0.05$, the drawing stress is 121.7~MPa. The values for all runs are summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:drawstress}.
% src: verification_recalc.py (typical run 1020_2a_24_rd_1000_cal_50_v_10_f_050: 121.7 MPa at
% Rf = 16.07 mm); surface PEEQ keeps rising for 10-20 mm past the cone exit.

Two of the three relations compared below are classical. In the slab-method form of Siebel,
\begin{equation}
\sigma_d = \sigma_f\left[\ln\frac{1}{1-r}\,(1+\mu\cot\alpha) + \frac{2}{3}\,\alpha\right],
\label{eq:siebel}
\end{equation}
with $r = 1-(R_f/R_0)^2$ the reduction in area, $\alpha$ the die semi-angle and $\sigma_f$ the flow
stress, and, in the upper-bound form of Avitzur,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\sigma_d = \sigma_f\biggl[\,&2f(\alpha)\ln\frac{R_0}{R_f}
  + \frac{2}{\sqrt3}\,\frac{\alpha-\sin\alpha\cos\alpha}{\sin^2\alpha}\\
  &+ \frac{2}{\sqrt3}\,m\cot\alpha\ln\frac{R_0}{R_f}
  + \frac{2}{\sqrt3}\,m\,\frac{L}{R_f}\biggr],
\end{split}
\label{eq:avitzur}
\end{equation}
whose four terms are the ideal deformation work, the redundant shear, the friction on the cone and
the friction on the land. Here $f(\alpha)$ is the closed form of the deformation integral,
% src: code/analytical.py f_avitzur against f_avitzur_numeric (agreement to eight digits)
$f\to1$ as $\alpha\to0$, so that the first
term reduces to $\ln[1/(1-r)]$, $m$ is the friction factor and $L$ the land length
\citep{siebel1947drahtziehen,avitzur1968metal,avitzur1971study}. In the model the land length is
$L = kD_0$, so that $L/R_f = kD_0/R_f$ is 2.2--2.3 times the land ratio.
\pending{$L = kD_0$ is read from the exports: the die reference point shifts by exactly
$36k$~mm, and the phase in which only the land is in contact lasts $36k$~mm. This is to be confirmed
against the model generator.}
% src: verify_land (die RP initial z = c - 36.000 k mm at every alpha; land-only tail phase
% 35.99 k - 0.84 mm over 88 runs; 36.9 mm at k = 1 in the earlier export at Q = 1.5 %).

Equation~\eqref{eq:avitzur} measures the friction through the factor $m$, the ratio of the
interface shear stress to the shear yield stress, whereas the simulations are driven by a Coulomb
coefficient $\mu$. The Siebel relation above already takes $\mu$ directly. The conversion
$m = \sqrt3\,\mu$ holds only where the normal pressure on the contact equals the flow stress, so it
is an assumption about the contact rather than a change of units. Avitzur gives the bound in a
second form in which $\mu$ enters directly and the land is treated as a bearing along which the
axial stress grows under Coulomb friction \citep{avitzur1968metal,avitzur1971study}. In the writing
of Vega et~al.\ \citep{vega2009temperature,vega2009investigation,haddi2011analysis}, who
build their modification on it, it reads
\begin{equation}
\frac{\sigma_d}{\sigma_f} = \frac{\begin{aligned}
  &2f(\alpha)\ln\frac{R_0}{R_f}
  + \frac{2}{\sqrt3}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sin^2\alpha}-\cot\alpha\right)\\
  &+ 2\mu\left[\cot\alpha\left(1-\ln\frac{R_0}{R_f}\right)\ln\frac{R_0}{R_f}
  + \frac{L}{R_f}\right]\end{aligned}}
  {\displaystyle 1+2\mu\,\frac{L}{R_f}},
\label{eq:avitzur-vega}
\end{equation}
written here for zero back tension and with the flow stress in place of the yield stress of the
original. The denominator is Avitzur's treatment of the land
\citep{avitzur1964large,avitzur1968metal}. It is what the axial force balance on a cylindrical
land under Coulomb friction gives when the normal pressure follows from the yield condition,
$p = \sigma_f-\sigma_z$, and is evaluated at the exit stress:
$\sigma_z(L) = \sigma_z(0) + 2\mu\,(L/R_f)\,[\sigma_f-\sigma_z(L)]$, which rearranges to the form
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:avitzur-vega}. Integrating the same balance along the land instead gives
$\sigma_z(L) = \sigma_f-[\sigma_f-\sigma_z(0)]\exp(-2\mu L/R_f)$. The two agree to first order in
$2\mu L/R_f$, and at the present parameters the integrated form lies above
Eq.~\eqref{eq:avitzur-vega} by at most 4.4\,\%. Both forms thus carry an explicit assumption about
the pressure on the land, which the exported reactions can test.

The modification those authors propose is to drop the assumption that $\mu$ is a constant of the
lubricant and make it a function of the temperature measured by a thermocouple set in the die above
the bearing, which they take to be proportional to the temperature of the wire--die interface,
\begin{equation}
\mu = \mu_0\left(\frac{T}{T_0}\right)^{m_\mu},
\label{eq:mu-temperature}
\end{equation}
with $\mu_0$ the coefficient at a reference temperature $T_0$.

Inverting Eq.~\eqref{eq:avitzur-vega} against their own force measurements, they obtain
$\mu_0 = 0.0125$,
$m_\mu = 2.68$ and $T_0 = 15.6\,^\circ$C for copper drawn in oil at 1--7~m/s, the temperatures
entering the ratio in degrees Celsius. In the present simulations $\mu$ is prescribed and constant,
so Eq.~\eqref{eq:mu-temperature} has no counterpart in the model. What it contributes to the
comparison is stated below.

For each run the three relations are evaluated with one and the same flow stress, obtained from
the constitutive equation~\eqref{eq:johnson-cook} by averaging it along the strain, strain-rate
and temperature histories exported for that run (Section~\ref{sec:materials}). It lies between
314 and 331~MPa depending on the run, and no parameter is fitted. Within a run $\sigma_f$
multiplies each relation as a whole, so the columns of Table~\ref{tab:drawstress} differ only in
the bracketed work terms, and an error in $\sigma_f$ would shift all three predictions of that
run by the same factor.
Below the individual runs, Table~\ref{tab:drawstress} gives for each relation the mean absolute
deviation from the finite-element value over all 111 runs and the secant slope
$\mathrm{d}\sigma_d/\mathrm{d}\mu$ between $\mu = 0.025$ and $\mu = 0.1$ at $\alpha = 12^\circ$.

The flow stress and the temperature rise can also be estimated from the geometry of the pass
alone, with no quantity taken from the finite-element model:
\begin{align}
\Delta &= \frac{R_0+R_f}{R_0-R_f}\,\sin\alpha, \label{eq:delta}\\
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{hom}} &= 2\ln\frac{R_0}{R_f}, \qquad
\Phi = 0.8 + \frac{\Delta}{4.4}, \qquad
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}} = \Phi\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{hom}}, \label{eq:closed-strain}\\
\edot &= \frac{6\,v\,R_f^2\tan\alpha\,\ln(R_0/R_f)}{R_0^3-R_f^3}, \qquad
\langle\sigma_f\rangle = A + \frac{B\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\,n}}{n+1}, \label{eq:closed-rate}\\
\Delta T &= \frac{\beta\,\sigma_d}{\rho c}, \label{eq:closed-rest}
\end{align}
where $\Delta$ is the inhomogeneity parameter of the deformation zone, the ratio of its mean
diameter to the length of contact along the die cone, set by the die semi-angle and the entry and
exit radii, $\Phi$ the excess-work factor, $\edot$ the mean strain rate over the time taken to
traverse the cone at constant volume flow, and $\langle\sigma_f\rangle$ the plastic-work-weighted
mean of the strain term of the constitutive equation, taken before its rate and temperature
multipliers \citep{backofen1972deformation}. The
excess-work correlation is quoted in the literature in more than one form. Replacing
$\Phi = 0.8+\Delta/4.4$ by the alternative $\Phi = 0.88+0.12\,\Delta$ changes $\Phi$ by a factor of
1.1 to 1.3 but the flow stress, and the estimated temperature rise, by only 0.4 to 1.1\,\%,
because the hardening term of the constitutive equation is small against $A$. The
choice therefore does not carry the comparison. The temperature
does not need a separate source, because it closes on the deformation work itself: that work per
unit volume is the part of $\sigma_d$ raised in the cone, a fraction $\beta$ of which becomes
heat, so the mean rise is $\Delta T = \beta\,\sigma_d/(\rho c)$. Since $\sigma_d$ depends on the
flow stress and the flow stress on the temperature, this is a fixed point, reached in at most
seven iterations. The friction on the land is left out of this balance, which is consistent
with the finding below that the land carries no load in the steady phase of these runs. For the
111 runs the fixed point returns a flow stress of 313--336~MPa, in agreement with the
314--331~MPa used in Table~\ref{tab:drawstress}, at $\Delta = 1.7$--4.7,
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0.30$--0.45 and $\edot = 2.4$--10.0~s$^{-1}$. The closed
estimate is quoted with its own value throughout, and no column of Table~\ref{tab:drawstress}
uses it.

The finite-element drawing stress does not depend on the land ratio: over $k$ from 0 to 1 it
changes by at most 0.4\,\% at every semi-angle, friction coefficient and speed, whereas the land
terms of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:avitzur} and \eqref{eq:avitzur-vega} raise the prediction from $k=0$ to

radial reactions of a Coulomb contact stand in the ratio $\tan(\alpha+\arctan\mu)$, on the land in
the ratio $\mu$. Splitting the reaction accordingly leaves the land with less than 0.3\,\% of the
axial force in the steady phase, and the whole reaction on the cone. The wire leaves the cone
still thinning and passes the land with a clearance of 0.04--0.27~mm. The land comes into contact
only at the end of the pass, when the tail of the billet crosses it, and then the ratio of the
reactions equals the prescribed $\mu$. Both relations therefore put a load on the land that the
model does not carry, and Table~\ref{tab:drawstress} compares them without their land terms. With
those terms the mean deviation rises to 43.5\,\% for Eq.~\eqref{eq:avitzur} and to 26.3\,\% for

analytical comparison. The friction coefficient is recovered from the data rather than read from

carried by the cone alone, which is the direct evidence that the land is unloaded.

The region of the design covered by these runs limits what the comparison establishes. At a
reduction of 10--15\,\% the inhomogeneity parameter is 1.7 to 4.7, close to the range of 1 to 3
in which these solutions are normally applied and against which they have been validated, and
the ideal work accounts for 46 to 68\,\% of the predicted drawing stress. The deviations of
Table~\ref{tab:drawstress} therefore characterise the closed-form solutions where they are
expected to hold: 10--17\,\% above the numerical solution, with the upper bound above the slab
estimate. What the comparison adds is the
treatment of the land. In this model a land of any length carries no load in steady drawing, so
a closed-form solution that loads the land will over-predict the drawing force in proportion to
the land length, and the land ratio is expected to have little effect on the drawing force.

2--5\,\% of the axial force, and it is to be confirmed with the model author.}
